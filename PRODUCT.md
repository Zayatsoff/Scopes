# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

ios

## Users

Ottawa residents who want a fast, trustworthy read on their city without digging through a dozen official sources and social feeds — weather, police activity, traffic, and local news. They open Scopes for quick reassurance and awareness: a commute check before leaving, "is anything happening near me," a daily news skim. The relationship is glance-and-go, not deep engagement.

## Product Purpose

Scopes consolidates Ottawa's essential daily civic signals — city service status, weather, traffic, police updates, and curated local news — into one fast, honest dashboard. It exists because that information is scattered across official city pages, weather services, police feeds, and news outlets. Success is a resident opening the app, understanding their city's status in one glance, and trusting that everything shown is real — never fabricated or placeholder content standing in for data the app doesn't actually have.

## Positioning

One glance, everything local: every real signal that affects a resident's day in Ottawa — weather, traffic, police, city services, news — in one fast, honest glance. No noise, no fake data.

## Brand Personality

Calm, trustworthy, local. The voice is civic and reassuring, not alarmist, even where the content itself is urgent (police activity, weather warnings, road closures). References that capture the right feel: Citymapper's visual polish, Apple News' article layout, CARROT Weather's severity/alert communication (clear urgency without inducing anxiety), Nextdoor's feed and tag/category filtering patterns, and Citizen's approach to police/safety alerts. Time-of-day greeting personalization (theSkimm, Apple Fitness "Good morning" headers) should feel warm and human — the one explicit guardrail from the brand conversation is that this kind of personalization must stay understated and never read as gimmicky.

## Anti-references

No specific bad-example apps were named. The one concrete guardrail: personalization touches (greeting headers, time-of-day copy) that feel gimmicky or attention-seeking rather than quietly useful.

## Design Principles

- Real data only, always — never fabricate, mock, or placeholder content that's presented as if it were real (this is why the Hydro tab links out to the official outage map instead of shipping simulated alerts).
- Glanceable over exhaustive — the home screen answers "is my city OK" in seconds; depth (full alert lists, article detail, filters) is one tap away, not the default view.
- Calm authority in alerts — severity and urgency are communicated clearly, closer to CARROT Weather's model, without tipping into alarm fatigue or panic framing.
- One city, no false choice — Ottawa-only by design; avoid UI that implies optionality that doesn't exist (the removed city dropdown was exactly this mistake).
- Personalization stays understated — greeting/time-of-day touches should feel human and quietly considered, never gimmicky.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA baseline. Because several surfaces carry safety-relevant information (weather warnings, police alerts, traffic incidents), severity and category must never be conveyed by color alone — pair with icon, label, or text. Standard AA expectations apply throughout: contrast, reduced-motion alternatives for animated transitions, text scaling, and screen-reader labels on icon-only controls.
